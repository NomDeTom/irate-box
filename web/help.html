🛟 Quick help

What this is, and how to get around.

You are connected to an Irate-Box. It is a small computer that serves these pages over its own network. It does not need internet access. Some apps can need it.

The administrator sets which apps and accounts you can use. You can use many apps without an account. If an app asks for a sign-in, go to Account.

To use HTTPS, see the box's certificate page.

📖 The guide explains how to set up and look after this box.

What is on the box

✏️
Excalidraw — whiteboard

Sketch diagrams by hand. To draw together, use Live collaboration (the people icon) and share the room link. Save to hub gallery in the menu keeps a copy on the box. Export downloads one to your device.

📈
Mermaid — diagrams as text

Type a flowchart, sequence diagram or chart and see it drawn. The Hub tab in History saves to the box. Load note pulls a diagram out of a Notes page. The docs buttons open the Mermaid book in the library.

📚
Kiwix — the offline library

Whole reference books on the box: a selection of Wikipedia, the Meshtastic and Mermaid documentation, and other books that the administrator adds. Search covers every book at once.

📝
Notes — a shared notebook

Markdown pages everyone here can read and edit. Good for agendas, lists, and anything worth keeping after the room empties.

🔀
Git — repositories

Browse the box's repositories and clone them over the network: git clone http://<this box>/git/<name>.git. Pushing needs permission. The administrator sets who can push.

📡
Meshtastic

A log analyser for Meshtastic radios, their documentation, and tools. Saved logs work in any browser, here. Reading a radio live over USB needs Chrome or Edge on a laptop, and the downloaded copy (Meshtastic → "Serial terminal, as a download"): browsers let USB serial work only on a page opened from your own machine, not on one served over this box's plain connection. Save it once, open it from your downloads, and plug the radio in.

🧮
Calculators

RF and LoRa, electronics, and general converters — all working offline.

💬
Shoutbox and board

On the front page. The shoutbox is quick chat. The box deletes shoutbox messages after one day. The board holds threads. The box deletes a thread one week after its last reply. Both periods count the time that the box is switched on.

🗨️
IRC chat

Live chat in any IRC app, if the administrator has added it. How to connect: the app has to be on your device already, as there is no internet here to get one.

📥
File drop

Leave a file for other people here, up to 25 MB, and pick up their files. The box deletes a file after one day of box on-time. Anyone on this network can download a file.

Getting around

  • The tiles on the front page open each app in a new tab, under a thin hub bar: 🏠 Hub brings you back, 🛟 shows a short reminder of all this, ☀️ 🌙 sets light or dark for everything, and ↗ opens the app on its own. The bar slides away as you scroll down.
  • A greyed-out tile is a service that is not running now, or that is not installed on this box.
  • If a button inside an app says it cannot connect, the feature usually needs internet access. The box has no internet access unless the administrator connects it.
  • To bring someone else in, show them the Join QR code on the front page.

Your work

  • Most apps keep what you are doing in your browser. It is still there next time you come back on the same device, and nobody else sees it.
  • Saving to the hub (the gallery, Notes, a shared drawing) puts it on the box, where everyone connected can see it.
  • Space on the box is limited, and the oldest shared work makes room for new. Download anything you want to keep.

Privacy and manners

  • Without an account, a page asks for any name. The box does not check that name. With an account, your username identifies you.
  • Anyone on this network can read what you share here, unless the app limits who can open it. Share only what you accept that other people can read.
  • The administrator can remove posts and saved work.

Why times look odd

The box has no clock battery. It does not always know the date. Ages such as "5 min ago" count the time that the box has been switched on. A message from yesterday can look newer when the box was off overnight.

HTTPS: the padlock

The box can serve its pages over HTTPS. HTTPS prevents other people on the same WiFi from reading or changing what you send, such as a password. Your phone or computer must trust the box first. Install the box's certificate once. The certificate page has the steps for each kind of device. The box's certificate authority is limited to this box's names. A device that enforces the limit rejects other sites. On Android, several screens and a warning appear. Without the certificate, the pages still work over plain HTTP.

For the administrator
